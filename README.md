# LightningCCP

Slash-command Discord bot for competitive programmers, modeled on [TLE](https://github.com/cheran-senthil/TLE).

## Setup

1. Create a bot at https://discord.com/developers/applications. Invite it with the `bot` and `applications.commands` scopes. No privileged intents needed.
2. `cp .env.example .env` and set `BOT_TOKEN`.
3. `uv sync`
4. `uv run python -m src`

### Docker

```
cp .env.example .env   # set BOT_TOKEN
docker compose up -d --build
docker compose logs -f
```

The SQLite database lives in the `bot-data` named volume, so it survives rebuilds. Update with `git pull && docker compose up -d --build`. `docker compose down` keeps the data; add `-v` to delete it.

## Commands

| Command | What it does |
|---|---|
| `/help [topic]` | A private guide: no topic gives an overview; topics are start, problems, matches, contests, ratings and contest-list |
| `/handle link <handle>` | Link a Codeforces handle; prove it by submitting a compile error |
| `/handle show [member] [handle]` | Profile of a member (default: you) or of whoever linked a handle |
| `/handle unlink` | Remove your link |
| `/gimme [rating] [min_rating] [max_rating] [tags] [exclude_tags] [contest_id]` | Random problem, hides ones you solved; `contest_id` limits it to one contest; with no rating arguments and no contest it aims at your match rating |
| `/match solo [rating] [tags] [exclude_tags]` | Practice alone; solving raises your match rating |
| `/match duel [opponent] [rating] [tags] [exclude_tags]` | 1v1 duel; name an opponent or let anyone join |
| `/match custom players [rating] [tags] [exclude_tags]` | Match for 2-10 players; the first to solve wins and everyone else loses, with no placing among them |
| `/match history [member] [kind]` | Your last 100 finished matches and your last 50 finished mashups, newest first, with your place; with a member, also your record and matches against them (private). `kind: matches` or `contests` shows only that kind (your record against a member counts matches only, so `contests` leaves it out) |
| `/host contest [min_rating] [max_rating] [count] [hour] [minute] [penalty] [teams] [players] [tags] [exclude_tags] [rated]` | Timed contest of several problems (default 5 problems, 800-1500, 2h, no penalty) with a live scoreboard. `rated` (default True) changes match ratings; `rated: False` makes an unrated contest that never changes ratings (the lobby and the results say "unrated"). Every contest is a team contest: `teams` 2-10 (default 10) and `players` per team 1-5 (default 1). With 1 player per team everyone is their own team: Join puts you in the first free team and the scoreboard shows players. With more, Join asks for a team number and the scoreboard shows teams. The host starts in team 1. At most 50 players in all (10 teams of 5) |
| `/host virtual <contest_id> [penalty] [teams] [players] [rated]` | `contest_id` autocompletes the newest finished contests by id or name (only ones whose problems the bot knows). Replays a finished Codeforces contest: the lobby, Start, scoreboard and Rematch work as in a normal contest, with the same `teams` and `players` options, but the problems are that contest's rated problems (at most 10) and the contest lasts as long as the real one. Problems you solved before do not matter, only submissions after Start count. `rated` works as in `/host contest` |
| `/host bingo [size] [mode] [win] [min_rating] [max_rating] [hour] [minute] [teams] [players] [tags] [exclude_tags] [rated]` | Bingo: teams race for the squares of one shared `size` x `size` board (3-7, default 5; problems rated `min_rating` to `max_rating`, default 800-1500, spread over the board; default 2h). The lobby, teams, Start, Check, Give Up, Rematch and `rated` work as in `/host contest`. The team that solves a problem first claims its square. `win: line` (default): the first team to hold a full row, column or diagonal wins and the bingo ends at once; `win: continuous`: it runs until time is up and ranks by lines held, then squares held, and ties go to the team that reached the score first. `mode: classic` (default): a claimed square stays claimed for good, and a full board ends the game. `mode: replace`: a claimed square gets a new problem rated 100 higher (never above `max_rating`; when nothing higher fits, the same rating) that nobody in the lobby had solved before the start; it counts from the claim, and the team that solves it takes the square over, so a line can be broken. The message has a solve log (the newest claims with the time, problem, team and the player who solved it), and its thread also names the square's new problem in replace mode. Teams and players work as in `/host contest` (up to 10 teams of 5, 50 players). Ratings change as in a contest |
| `/ff` | Leave your unfinished match or contest: from a lobby you leave it (the host leaving cancels it), from a running one you give up (private). It works even when the buttons of the old message are out of reach |
| `/profile [member] [handle]` | One private card: match rating and rank in this server, peak rating, games played (matches and contests) with how many raised, lowered or left your rating, daily streak and Codeforces rating (default: you; `handle` finds the member who linked that Codeforces handle) |
| `/stats [sort] [kind]` | Everyone ranked by match rating, 10 per page with Prev and Next buttons (`sort: streak` ranks by daily streak instead; `kind: matches` or `contests` counts only that kind, rating and games included; default both, shown as games); your own row is bold and the footer gives your rank; a fire emoji and number marks a daily streak (private) |
| `/plot rating [member] [handle] [member2..member5]` | Graph of a member's match rating in this server (private; default: you). Add `member2` to `member5` to draw up to 5 members on one graph with a legend (not with `handle`); members with no finished matches are named and left out |
| `/plot codeforces [member] [handle] [handle2..handle5] [member2..member5]` | Graph of a Codeforces rating history, any handle works (private; default: you). Add `handle2` to `handle5` (any handles) and `member2` to `member5` (their linked handles) to draw up to 5 lines in all on one graph with a legend; handles with no rated contest are named and left out. It makes two Codeforces calls per handle, so five handles take about ten seconds |
| `/contests upcoming\|running\|recent` | Paginated contest lists |
| `/daily [rating]` | Your daily problem, rated your match rating floored to 100 (or the `rating` you pick; picking a different rating than today's unsolved daily swaps in a new problem); solve it on Codeforces, run `/daily` again to claim double rating. New problem every 00:00 UTC; shows your daily streak |
| `/stalk [handle] [member]` | Problems someone tried but never solved (private; default: you) |
| `/ranklist contest_id` | Official standings of this server's linked members in a contest (private) |
| `/channel reminder [set] [role] [before] [exclude] [clear] [clear_role]` | Where contest reminders go (needs Manage Server). `set` is the channel, `role` a role to ping, `before` the minutes before the start (default 10,60), `exclude` a regex on the contest name (case-insensitive, up to 100 characters; matching contests get no reminder; type `clear` to remove it), `clear` stops reminders (it forgets the channel only), `clear_role` stops the role ping. Every option is optional and you can set several at once; with none it shows the settings |
| `/channel recap [set] [clear]` | Where the bot posts, after each contest ends, how your linked members placed (top 10). Separate from the reminder channel, so set both if you want both; with no option it shows the channel |

A match is a public message. `tags` and `exclude_tags` work like in `/gimme` and limit the problem the match picks (the lobby shows them, and a rematch keeps them). The lobby has Join, Start and Leave buttons; once the host presses Start, the problem appears with Check and Give Up buttons. A finished duel shows a Rematch button: either player can press it to open a new duel lobby against the same opponent, who then presses Join (the old rating argument is reused). Everyone starts at a match rating of 800. With no `rating` argument, `/gimme` and `/match` pick a problem at your match rating (rounded to 100; the players' average for duel and custom). After a match, ratings change. Solo only counts solving, so giving up or running out of time never lowers your rating: you gain +1 when the problem is 400 or more below your rating, +10 when equal and +33 when 400 or more above (linear in between). Duel and custom compare every pair of players by solve order, and the winner gains a fixed step by how far the loser is rated from them: +16 when the loser is 400 or more below, +33 from 200 below, +49 within 200 of each other, +67 from 200 above, +95 from 400 above. The loser loses the same, and a tie moves nothing. A custom match ends at the first solve (or when everyone gave up or time runs out): the first solver beats every other player, the others tie, and each player's change is the average of those duels times 1.5 (for example +74 for the winner and -25 for each loser in a four-player match of equals). Its results and history show a winner instead of places.

The daily bonus doubles one rating change per UTC day: either the `/daily` problem, or your first solved solo match of the day (which then counts as that day's daily; an unsolved solo match does not use it up). Each claimed day adds one to your daily streak. Missing one day keeps the streak; missing two days in a row resets it to zero.

A contest from `/host` opens a lobby with Join, Start and Leave buttons (the lobby lasts 10 minutes; if the host leaves it is cancelled). When the host presses Start, the bot picks `count` problems spread evenly (easy to hard) across the rating range that nobody in the lobby has solved, and nobody can join any more. Players solve on Codeforces; the bot reads submissions made between the start and the end and updates the scoreboard (running contests share half of the Codeforces call rate, the player read longest ago first; the fewer players are waiting, the shorter the round: every 10 seconds for a few players, up to every minute when more than 30 wait, so a 50 player contest updates each player about every 100 seconds; the Check button reads you at once; when time runs out everyone is read), which ranks by problems solved, then total time: minutes from the start for each solve plus `penalty` minutes for every wrong attempt before it (compilation errors are free; default penalty 0). The message turns into the final standings when time runs out, and a rated contest changes match ratings the way a custom match does: every pair of players is a duel decided by the standings (equal standings tie), and a player's change is the average over the others. Each team counts as one player rated at its members' average (a team of one at its player's rating), and every member gets the team's change. A contest with a single team, and every contest hosted with `rated: False`, does not change ratings. A user can be in only one unfinished match or contest per server at a time. While a contest runs, the Give Up button leaves it: the player keeps their place and rating change from the solves so far (their later submissions are not read), and is free to join another match or contest. A contest ends early, with the rating changes, when every player still in has solved every problem (or everyone gave up). A problem counts for a team once, from the teammate who solved it first (their time and their wrong attempts), and the standings rank teams by problems solved, then total time. Every `/host` message (contest, virtual and bingo) gets a thread where each solve that counts is posted with its time, problem, team and the player who solved it (and the wrong attempts before it); the newest thread message carries the current board (the standings, or the bingo grid) and older ones keep only their text. A player who gives up (Give Up or `/ff`) is noted in the thread. When the contest ends (or is cancelled) the thread gets a closing message with the winner and the final standings. The bot needs the Create Public Threads and Send Messages in Threads permissions; without them the contest still works but has no thread. A finished contest has a Rematch button: any of its players can press it to open a new lobby with the same settings and become its host (the host starts in team 1). In a contest with more than one player per team, press Join again and enter another team number to change team (the host stays in team 1).
